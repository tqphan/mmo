# 0033: Shipyards are public rows built stage by stage; a vehicle at 0 HP sinks on a timer, drops its cargo for anyone, and comes back to its owner as a wreck to mend

- **Status:** Accepted, 2026-10-31
- **Context:**
  - Vehicles B (BACKLOG M1, docs/design/vehicles.md §2B) needs the Swiftgull built at a shipyard, vehicles destroyed with their cargo falling, repairs, and a way to stow a vehicle that's out of sight (ADR 0031's open consequence).
  - In the original, a design and a fee placed a dry dock in deep water. Lumber, iron and fabric packs went in, in that order, for 25 labor each, then three more 25-labor steps, and the ship item appeared in the bag. A destroyed ship's item stayed with its owner and was restored with a consumable and a wait of 10–30 minutes. Its packs sank where it went down, for anyone to take. Ships couldn't be dismissed in combat. How ships were repaired at sea wasn't found.
- **Decision:**
  - **Content:** shipyards and their stages are a `[[shipyard]]` table in `ships.toml` (design item, sea hull, placing labor, and stages each with labor and an optional pack; the last stage launches and takes no pack). `[vehicles]` holds the mending item, repair labor and share, the under-fire time and the sinking time; hulls gain `mend_kits` and `mend_s`. A new item kind, `design`. All of it is in the content hash.
  - **A shipyard is a public Static-tier row** (`shipyard`): kind, owner, a footprint (its hull's deck, placed where that hull would be summoned and afloat there) and the **number of stages done**. `work_shipyard` does the next stage for whoever is in reach, taking the pack it names off their back and their labor, except the last stage, which only the owner's account may do and which grants the hull's item to the owner's bag and deletes the row. One yard per character. Yards can't be attacked yet.
  - **All damage to a vehicle goes through `ship::damage`.** It stamps a public `vehicle.hit_at`, so the vehicle is **under fire** for `under_fire_s` (no stowing, no repairs). At 0 HP the helm is let go and a private scheduled `vehicle_wreck` row fires `sink_s` later. Clients draw the hull settling meanwhile, from the moment they see 0 HP.
  - **The wreck** lays each cargo pack as a public `dropped_pack` at its slot's world point (floating at sea level off a ship, on the ground off a cart), puts riders off as stowing does, and moves the owner's item back into the bag (or `RETURNS` if it's full) with a private **`wreck`** row (`my_wrecks`). Summoning is refused until `mend_vehicle` has taken the hull's kits and set `ready_at`, which is compared with the clock and never ticked. `pick_up_pack` now works while swimming, so floating packs can be taken.
  - **Repairs** (`repair_vehicle`) are ours: anyone aboard or beside a vehicle spends one kit and some labor for a share of its HP, refused under fire.
  - **`my_vehicles`**, a view over `vehicle` by a new `owner` index, lets the client stow a vehicle it can't see.
- **Consequences:**
  - The schema changed (`vehicle.hit_at`, the `owner` index, new tables `shipyard`, `vehicle_wreck` and `wreck`, the views): run `cargo xtask publish --clear` and then `cargo xtask world` once.
  - Packs floating at the surface, not on the seabed, are a deviation until characters can dive. A wreck held in `RETURNS` (a full bag) can't be mended until mail delivers it (M2).
  - Only cannons and dev damage hit vehicles; skills and NPCs don't yet. Repairs under fire being refused keeps a fight from being out-healed with kits; the M1 playtest may tune `under_fire_s` and `repair_pct`.
